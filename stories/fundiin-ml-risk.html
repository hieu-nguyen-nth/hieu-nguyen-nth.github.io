Fundiin / APPLIED ML · RISK OPERATIONS

Scale risk screening with ML and human judgment

Make ML useful inside a risk decision system, with the right evaluation and human boundaries.

ML-based screening reduced manual-review workload by 50% across a $2M monthly lending portfolio. Data Science owned model development; my contribution connected business framing, evaluation, workflow design, and deployment.

Read alongside my career overview ↗

ContextDecisionExecutionResult

The constraint

Fundiin’s rule-based screening was becoming harder to maintain, and Risk Operations still carried a substantial manual-review workload. The opportunity was to use combinations of transaction, merchant, device, behavioral, and customer signals more effectively. Overall accuracy would not be a sufficient launch criterion: rare fraud creates class imbalance, and false positives can reject good customers while missed fraud creates real losses.

The decision I made

I brought Risk, Data Science, and Engineering together around the operating outcome and the economics of errors. We defined the model’s role, policy constraints, thresholds, and manual-review bands. With limited data-science capacity, we selected AutoML for the initial production iteration so the team could learn from the decision workflow without first building a custom training stack. The tradeoff was less transparency and control, making validation and oversight especially important.

THE PRODUCT LOGIC

  1. 01Define risk goals & error costs
  2. 02Evaluate in shadow mode
  3. 03Set thresholds & human review
  4. 04Stage deployment & monitor outcomes

What I did

  1. Aligned on the cost of errors

    Brought Risk, Data Science, and Engineering together around prediction targets, precision/coverage tradeoffs, and the customer and financial costs of wrong decisions.

  2. Chose speed to learning over a custom stack

    With limited data-science capacity, selected AutoML for the first iteration. Data Science owned model training; I connected business features, policy constraints, and the operating workflow.

  3. Challenged misleading offline performance

    Asked whether proposed signals would have existed at the original decision time, surfacing potential information leakage without treating later-known outcomes as usable inputs.

  4. Validated before changing customer decisions

    Ran alongside the existing process in shadow mode for roughly two to three months, inspected disagreements, then staged rollout with human review for uncertain cases.

What changed

ML-based screening reduced manual-review workload by 50% across a $2M monthly lending portfolio. Data Science owned model development; my contribution connected business framing, evaluation, workflow design, and deployment.

The product is the complete decision system: model, policy rules, thresholds, exceptions, monitoring, and human accountability. My role was to connect those parts to a business outcome, not just introduce an algorithm.
