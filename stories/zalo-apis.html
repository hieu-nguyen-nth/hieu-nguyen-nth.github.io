Build enterprise integrations around reusable payment capabilities

Use enterprise launches to create repeatable product capabilities.

Across the enterprise portfolio, ride-hailing transactions grew 50%, payout volume grew 40%, and integrations acquired 100K users. These are portfolio results, not a claim that every partner delivered every metric.

The constraint

Enterprise partners needed different payment behaviors: collecting money, paying it out, or reserving funds before final settlement. Treating every request as another one-off integration would limit reuse and multiply operating complexity.

The decision I made

I separated these use cases into appropriate product flows and API capabilities. Partner prioritization considered reach and economics alongside whether the requested work would create a reusable capability for future integrations.

THE PRODUCT LOGIC

  1. 01Enterprise business model
  2. 02Payment capability & API contract
  3. 03Partner integration & certification
  4. 04Launch & operating feedback

What I did

  1. Translated business models into payment flows

    Worked with Business and Sales on partner opportunities, pitches, requirements, and solution design. Collection, disbursement, and pre-authorization needed different product models.

  2. Avoided forcing payouts into a collection model

    Defined the money-out journey with fund-flow, ledger, finance, and accounting requirements, rather than treating it as reversed checkout.

  3. Built shared capabilities with explicit exceptions

    Used common contracts and integration sequences where appropriate, while separating partner-specific experience and compliance requirements.

  4. Owned the path to a working launch

    Coordinated documentation, testing, partner readiness, operating contacts, go-live, and monitoring across both companies. Results describe the portfolio, not every named partner individually.

What changed

Across the enterprise portfolio, ride-hailing transactions grew 50%, payout volume grew 40%, and integrations acquired 100K users. These are portfolio results, not a claim that every partner delivered every metric.

An API product includes the partner’s path to successful use: clear flows, documentation, testing, operational contacts, and launch readiness—not just an endpoint.
