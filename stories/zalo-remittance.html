ZaloPay · VNG / NEW MARKET

Open a new cross-border product in six months

Build the domestic capability while partnering for international reach.

We launched a new remittance product and revenue stream in six months, establishing infrastructure for cross-border transfers.

Read alongside my career overview ↗

ContextDecisionExecutionResult

The constraint

ZaloPay had domestic wallet infrastructure but no international remittance product. Launching required more than an incoming transfer: partner onboarding, bank settlement, recipient payout, compliance, reconciliation, and support had to behave as one service.

The decision I made

We chose a partnership-led model for overseas origination and corridor reach, while building or extending the domestic wallet, bank connectivity, and operating capabilities. I helped connect the customer journey to the fund-flow and responsibility model.

THE PRODUCT LOGIC

  1. 01Overseas originating partner
  2. 02Bank & compliance coordination
  3. 03Domestic wallet capabilities
  4. 04Recipient payout & reconciliation

What I did

  1. Chose the ecosystem boundary

    Assessed partner reach and market opportunity. Used overseas partners for origination while extending ZaloPay’s domestic wallet, bank connectivity, and payout capabilities.

  2. Mapped the complete service

    Connected partner onboarding, fund flows, compliance, settlement, reconciliation, recipient payout, and support rather than treating launch as an API handoff.

  3. Stepped into a coordination gap

    Created one integrated plan when surrounding program ownership was stretched. Made dependencies, resource needs, and decisions requiring senior sponsorship explicit.

  4. Aligned teams beyond my reporting line

    Led delivery across engineering squads, design, legal, compliance, operations, finance, and business while keeping product and program responsibilities clear.

What changed

We launched a new remittance product and revenue stream in six months, establishing infrastructure for cross-border transfers.

Cross-company product delivery depends on making ownership and failure handling clear, especially when no single team controls the full journey.
