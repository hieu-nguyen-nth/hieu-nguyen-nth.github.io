Cake by VPBank / INTERNAL PLATFORM

Give Risk a control plane, not an engineering queue

Make policy change a product capability that Risk can operate.

Fraud controls could be deployed in about an hour instead of days, protecting products serving 700K+ monthly active users.

Read alongside my career overview ↗

ContextDecisionExecutionResult

The constraint

Risk controls were spread across product teams. Changing a policy meant coordinating engineering work and release schedules, even when the team needed to respond quickly to a new fraud pattern. Risk Operations was the internal customer whose workflow needed redesign.

The decision I made

I reframed the problem around a configurable control plane: common data models and rule workflows that Risk could operate across products. The initial scope prioritized rule and policy management; broader investigation tooling could follow.

THE PRODUCT LOGIC

  1. 01Risk policy
  2. 02Configurable rules
  3. 03Shared decision engine
  4. 04Controls across products

What I did

  1. Interviewed the internal customer

    Reviewed existing engines and Risk workflows to see where policy changes became engineering requests and release dependencies.

  2. Changed ownership of the bottleneck

    Translated policies and data variables into configurable rules, so Risk could operate controls rather than queue every change with a product team.

  3. Prototyped the control plane

    Built an admin prototype and aligned backend, mobile, web portal, card, Risk, and customer-service teams on the operating workflow.

  4. Protected the first release

    Prioritized rule and policy management. Kept investigation tooling and broader event aggregation on a follow-on path instead of delaying the core capability.

What changed

Fraud controls could be deployed in about an hour instead of days, protecting products serving 700K+ monthly active users.

An internal platform changes operating leverage when it gives the right team a usable capability, with clear ownership across the system.
